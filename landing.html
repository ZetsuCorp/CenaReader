How it works

Four steps, no timeline to edit, no manual character setup.
1

Add your keys

A free Groq key powers the speaker casting, a free ElevenLabs key powers real voices — both entered once, stored only on your device.

2

Paste your text

Any scene, chapter, or script — even prose with no dialogue tags at all.

3

We cast it

Every line gets a speaker, a confidence flag on anything ambiguous, and a read on its emotional tone.

4

Press play

Each character speaks in their own assigned voice, delivered in character — confused, excited, sarcastic, whatever the scene calls for.

What makes this different

Most narration tools read everything in one flat voice, or make you manually tag every line yourself.

Recast on the fly

Rename or reassign any character right in the transcript, no re-running the analysis.

You own the engine

Bring your own Groq and ElevenLabs keys — nothing routed through a paid subscription, swap providers anytime.

You can fix its mistakes

Rename a misattributed speaker with one click, right in the transcript, before you ever hit play. Nothing's locked in.

Built on real messy prose

Tested against writing that never says "she said," where narration just runs straight into a quote. Most narration tools assume clean dialogue tags; this one doesn't need them.

Built for cinematic audiobooks — and what comes after

Casting voices per character is the slow part of turning a story into anything animated. Automate that first, and a full cinematic audiobook becomes the fast on-ramp to an animated feature later — not a separate project starting from zero.

Where things stand today

This is an early version, and we'd rather tell you straight than oversell it: playback currently uses your browser's built-in voices for free, or your own ElevenLabs account for higher-quality ones — there's no built-in voice library or one-click export to MP3 yet. Casting and emotion-tagging are fully automatic and free to use on any passage. Full audiobook export is on the roadmap, not here yet.
